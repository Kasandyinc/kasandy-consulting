Your entire business
on one page.

The Business Model Canvas is a strategic planning tool that maps how your business creates, delivers, and captures value — all on a single page. It was developed by Alexander Osterwalder and is now used by startups, established businesses, and government agencies worldwide to align teams, clarify strategy, and communicate with partners and funders.

Why this matters for your business
  • Makes your business model visible — you can't fix what you can't see
  • Reveals the gaps: what resources do you have? What are you missing? What does your model actually cost?
  • Helps you communicate to investors, partners, funders, and buyers — in minutes, not hours
  • Creates a shared language for your team around how the business actually works
  • Is the foundation of a strong procurement capability statement, grant application, and investor pitch
Key Partners Left side
Who are your key suppliers and partners? Who do you need to work with to deliver your value proposition?
Ask: What can we not do alone?
Key Activities Left side
What activities do you perform every day to keep the business running and delivering value?
Ask: What must we do to stay in business?
Key Resources Left side
What assets — human, physical, intellectual, financial — does your business need to function?
Ask: What are we built on?
Value Proposition Centre
What problem do you solve? What value do you create for your customers? What makes you the right choice?
Ask: Why do customers choose us?
Customer Relationships Right side
How do you acquire, retain, and grow your customer base? What kind of relationship do customers expect?
Ask: How do we treat our customers?
Channels Right side
How does your value proposition reach your customers? What communication, distribution, and sales channels do you use?
Ask: How do we reach and deliver?
Customer Segments Right side
Who are your most important customers? What groups of people or organizations does your business serve?
Ask: Who are we really for?
Cost Structure Bottom left
What are the most significant costs in your business? Which key resources and activities are most expensive?
Ask: What does this business cost to run?
Tips for completing your canvas
Start with Value Proposition and Customer Segments. Everything else flows from knowing what you do and who you serve.
Be specific. "Good service" is not a value proposition. "48-hour delivery to hospitality buyers in Western Canada" is.
Use bullet points, not paragraphs. The canvas is meant to be scannable in under two minutes.
Revisit the canvas quarterly. Your model should evolve as your business does.
