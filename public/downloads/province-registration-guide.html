Every province and territory in Canada has its own registration process, fees, naming requirements, and compliance obligations. This guide gives you the exact steps, costs, and links for all 10 provinces and 3 territories — plus the federal registration options that apply everywhere.

How to use this guide: Find your province on pages 2–3. Complete the quick-start checklist on page 4 after registering. If you're unsure which business structure to use (sole proprietor, corporation, etc.) — start with the comparison on this page. When in doubt, consult an accountant or lawyer before incorporating.
Which business structure is right for you?
Sole Proprietorship
Simplest and cheapest to register. You are the business — personal and business liability are the same. Income reported on your personal tax return.
Best for: freelancers, consultants, early-stage testing
Partnership
Two or more people share ownership. Requires a partnership agreement. Each partner is personally liable for the business's debts — including what the other partner does.
Best for: co-founded services businesses with known, trusted partners
Corporation (Provincial or Federal)
Separate legal entity. Liability is limited to the company's assets. More complex to set up and maintain (annual filings, director requirements). Lower tax rate on corporate income.
Best for: growth businesses, government contracts, investors
Non-Profit / Charitable Org
Operates for a social purpose, not for profit. Can apply for charitable status with CRA. Different governance requirements (board of directors, bylaws).
Best for: community programs, advocacy, social enterprises

Western Canada & Territories — Step-by-Step
Federal Incorporation — Corporations Canada (Valid Everywhere)
When to choose federal
You plan to operate in multiple provinces
You want the word "Canada" in your name
You want maximum protection on your business name nationally
You're seeking investors or planning to go public
Steps to incorporate federally
Step 1: Name search — NUANS report ($13.80) at ised.canada.ca
Step 2: File Articles of Incorporation at corporationscanada.ic.gc.ca ($200 online)
Step 3: Register in each province where you operate (extra-provincial registration ~$50–$250 per province)
Step 4: Obtain Business Number from CRA (free, same day online)
Step 5: Register for GST/HST if annual revenue exceeds or will exceed $30,000
British ColumbiaBC
Registry
BC Registry Services — bcregistry.gov.bc.ca
Sole Prop: $40 · Corp: $350Same day
Sole Proprietorship / Partnership Steps
1. Choose your business name or use your legal name
2. Register at bcregistry.gov.bc.ca → Business Names
3. Obtain CRA Business Number (if needed)
4. Apply for any municipal business licence (city hall)
Corporation Steps (BC Business Corporations Act)
1. Name approval — Name Request online at bcregistry.gov.bc.ca ($30)
2. File Incorporation Application — BC Online system ($350)
3. Create Notice of Articles and Register Office address
4. Obtain Business Number from CRA
5. Register for PST with BC Ministry of Finance (if selling goods)

Sole Proprietorship / Partnership Steps

OntarioON
Registry
Ontario Business Registry (ServiceOntario) — ontario.ca/business
Sole Prop: $60 · Corp: $360Same day (online)
Sole Proprietorship / Partnership Steps
1. Register business name — Ontario Business Registry online ($60)
2. Registration valid for 5 years — must renew
3. HST registration with CRA (if applicable)
4. Municipal business licence (check your city)
Corporation Steps (Ontario Business Corporations Act)
1. Name search — NUANS report ($14) or numbered company (no name search needed)
2. File Articles of Incorporation — Ontario Business Registry ($360)
3. Register for HST with CRA
4. File initial notice of directors and registered office
5. Annual return — $25/year (due on anniversary of incorporation)
Note: Ontario corporations must file Ontario corporate tax (T2 + CT23) separately from federal
ontario.ca/business-registry
QuébecQC
Registry
Registraire des entreprises Québec (REQ) — registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca
Sole Prop: $38 · Corp: $3581–3 days
Important: Québec-Specific Requirements
All businesses must register with the REQ — even sole proprietors
French language: Business name must be in French, or bilingual with French equivalent. Application may need to be in French.
QST (Québec Sales Tax): Register separately with Revenu Québec (different from federal GST)
Québec corporate tax: File separately with Revenu Québec (not just CRA)
Key Steps
1. Name approval — verify French language compliance
2. Register at REQ (registreentreprises.gouv.qc.ca)
3. Register for QST with Revenu Québec
4. Annual declaration with REQ — required

Step 4
After Registration Checklist
After you register — don't stop here

What to do right after
registration.

Registration is step one. These are the next steps that keep your business compliant, credible, and procurement-ready.

Federal Tax Requirements
Business Number (BN) registered with CRA — cra-arc.gc.ca or 1-800-959-5525
Free and instant. Your BN is needed for almost every government form, contract, and funder application.
GST/HST account registered (if revenue exceeds or will exceed $30,000/year)
Even if below threshold, registering early is beneficial for claiming input tax credits.
Payroll account registered (if you have employees)
Provincial tax registration — PST (BC, SK, MB) or QST (QC) if applicable
Atlantic provinces use harmonized HST — no separate provincial registration needed.
Banking & Financial Setup
Separate business bank account opened — never mix business and personal funds
Required for government contracts. Most banks require your BN and registration documents.
Accounting software set up — Wave (free), QuickBooks, or FreshBooks
Business credit card applied for — start building business credit history
Procurement & Contracts
Registered on buyandsell.gc.ca — federal supplier database
Free. Required for all federal contracts. Enter your NAICS codes accurately.
Registered on provincial procurement portals relevant to your location
BC Bid, Biddingo (ON), Alberta Purchasing Connection, MERX, etc.
Commercial general liability insurance obtained — minimum $2M
Required before most government or corporate contracts. Shop around — rates vary significantly.
Capability statement drafted — see Kasandy Consulting's free Capability Statement Guide
Supplier diversity certification started — CAMSC, CCAB, WBE, BEBC Society
Process takes 4–12 weeks. Start early — many programs require a minimum of 6 months in operation.
Operations & Compliance
Municipal business licence obtained (required in most cities)
WSIB / WCB registration if applicable to your industry
Privacy policy drafted — required if collecting personal data (PIPEDA federally; provincial equivalents in AB, BC, QC)
Annual filing date noted in calendar — corporations must file annual returns
