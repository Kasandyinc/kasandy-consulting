Non-Profit Version

Your mission model
on one page.

The Business Model Canvas for non-profits maps how your organization creates social value, delivers programs, and sustains itself financially — all on a single page. Use it for board alignment, strategic planning, funder conversations, and staff onboarding.

How the non-profit version differs: Instead of "Revenue Streams," you have Outcome Streams (financial and non-financial). Instead of "Customer Segments," you have Beneficiaries and Partners. The Value Proposition focuses on mission impact, not profit. Everything else maps to the same framework.
Key Partners Left side
Government funders, foundations, healthcare systems, community organizations, shipping partners, pro bono service providers.
Who helps you deliver the mission you couldn't deliver alone?
Key Activities Left side
Program delivery, fundraising, partnerships, training, government liaison, communications, charity events, impact evaluation.
What does your team do every week to keep the mission alive?
Key Resources Left side
Board, staff, volunteers, physical space, databases, intellectual property, website, financial reserves, donor relationships.
What do you have that you depend on to operate?
Value Proposition Centre
What is the specific problem you solve, for whom, and how? What does the community look like because your organization exists?
If you disappeared tomorrow, what would be lost?
Relationships Right side
Community trust, accountability, partnerships, donor stewardship, beneficiary engagement, board governance.
How do you build and sustain trust with everyone you serve?
Channels Right side
Website, social media, direct outreach, community events, media relations, board networks, partner referrals, email.
How do people find you, and how do you reach them?
Beneficiaries & Partners Right side
Who does your work directly benefit? Who are your funders and institutional partners? Who are your advocates and volunteers?
Who are you accountable to, and who do you serve?
Outcome Streams Bottom right
Financial (grants, donations, earned revenue, memberships) and non-financial (lives impacted, programs delivered, systems changed).
What does success look like — in numbers and in the world?
Tips for non-profit leaders
Use this canvas in your board strategic planning retreat. Fill it together — you'll surface misalignment faster than any agenda item.
Funders love seeing a completed canvas. It signals strategic clarity — and organizations with that clarity get funded more reliably.
The Cost Structure section is where most non-profits under-represent themselves. Be honest about what it actually costs to deliver the mission.
Revisit annually. A three-year-old canvas that hasn't changed is a sign that strategic thinking has stalled.
